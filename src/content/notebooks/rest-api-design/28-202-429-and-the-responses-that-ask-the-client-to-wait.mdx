Three codes for the cases where the honest answer is "not now".

**`202 Accepted`: I have taken this, and it has not happened yet.** The right
response to any action you queue rather than perform.

```text
   POST /organizations/8f2a/archive
   ──▶ 202 Accepted
       Location: /v1/jobs/44           ← where to poll for the outcome
       { "jobId": "44", "status": "queued" }
```

The lecture's own archive example is exactly this case. It observes that
archiving may need to cascade through projects, tasks and notifications, and
<Peach>once that work is measured in seconds, holding the connection open is the wrong
design</Peach>. `202` says the request was valid and accepted, and points at somewhere
to find out how it went. <Ref to="/large-scale-ingestion">Notebook 01</Ref> covers the queue behind it.

<Mint>The important part is what `202` does not promise: it does not promise the work
will succeed.</Mint> A client that treats `202` as success will report completion for
jobs that later fail, so the job resource must carry a real terminal state.

**`429 Too Many Requests`: you are going too fast.** Always with a `Retry-After`.

```text
   429 Too Many Requests
   Retry-After: 30                 ← seconds, or an HTTP date
   RateLimit-Limit: 1000
   RateLimit-Remaining: 0
   RateLimit-Reset: 30
```

<Peach>Without `Retry-After` you have told a client to back off without telling it how
far</Peach>, and the usual result is a tight retry loop that makes the overload worse.
The `RateLimit-*` family lets a well-behaved client slow down before it hits the
wall, which is better for both sides. <Ref to="/scaling">Notebook 06</Ref> <Ref to="#29-one-error-shape-everywhere">section 29</Ref> covers the limiting
algorithms; the interface obligation is simply to say the number.

**`503 Service Unavailable`: I am not able to serve this now.** Also takes
`Retry-After`. The distinction from `500` is worth being precise about:
<Mint>`500` means something broke and retrying will probably break again; `503`
means nothing is broken and retrying later will probably work.</Mint> Clients and
proxies treat them differently, and returning `500` for a temporary overload
suppresses the retry that would have succeeded.

<DoThis>

Any status that asks a client to come back later must say when. <Mint>`Retry-After`
on every `429` and every `503`, without exception.</Mint> It is the difference between
backpressure and a retry storm.

</DoThis>

<KeyIdea>

<Peach>`202` means accepted and unfinished, `429` and `503` mean come back later, and
all three are useless without somewhere to look or a time to wait.</Peach>

</KeyIdea>
