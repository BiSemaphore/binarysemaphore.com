title: "200, 201, and the Location header"
part: "IV"
partTitle: "The response is the other half of the interface"
---
The two success codes for something that produced a result.

```text
   200 OK          here is the thing you asked for, or the result of what
                   you asked me to do
   201 Created     a new resource now exists, and here it is
```

The lecture's demonstration is correct throughout: `201` for the create, `200`
for reads and for updates, and `200` when a custom action succeeded without
creating anything. <Peach>It also makes a point worth repeating, that you cannot infer
the status from the method.</Peach> A `POST` that archives returns `200`; a `POST` that
clones returns `201`, because a clone creates. The method says what kind of
request it is; the status says what happened.

**What the lecture omits is the header that belongs with `201`.** RFC 9110 says a
`201` response should carry a `Location` header giving the URL of the thing that
was created.

```text
   POST /v1/organizations
   ──▶  201 Created
        Location: /v1/organizations/8f2a       ← this line
        { "id": "8f2a", "name": "Acme", ... }
```

The body tells a human what happened. <Mint>The `Location` header tells a program
where the thing lives, in the one place every HTTP client already knows to
look</Mint>, without having to know that your identifier field is called `id` rather
than `uuid` or `organizationId`. It costs one line and it is the only part of a
`201` that is machine-readable without prior knowledge of your schema.

**Return the created representation in the body as well.** The specification does
not require it and the lecture does it, correctly. <Peach>If you return only a
`Location`, every client must immediately issue a `GET` to discover the
server-assigned fields</Peach>, which is a guaranteed second round trip on the most
common operation in your API.

<DoThis>

On `201`, send both: the `Location` header and the full created resource in the
body. <Mint>The header serves programs, the body saves a round trip, and neither
substitutes for the other.</Mint>

</DoThis>

<KeyIdea>

<Peach>`201` plus `Location` plus the representation.</Peach> The status says a thing was
made, the header says where it is, and the body means nobody has to ask again.

</KeyIdea>
