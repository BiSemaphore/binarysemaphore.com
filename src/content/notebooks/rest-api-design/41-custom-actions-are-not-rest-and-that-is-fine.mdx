title: "Custom actions are not REST, and that is fine"

The lecture presents custom actions as licensed by the standard: `POST` is
open-ended in the specification, so putting an action behind it is within the
rules. <Peach>The first half is true and the conclusion is a little too comfortable.</Peach>

`POST` is genuinely open-ended. RFC 9110 defines it as asking the target resource
to process the enclosed representation according to the resource's own semantics,
which permits nearly anything. What that licenses is `POST` doing arbitrary work.
It does not make `/organizations/8f2a/archive` a resource, and by the uniform
interface constraint from <Ref to="#3-fielding-s-six-constraints">section 3</Ref>, <Peach>a URL is supposed to identify a thing
rather than name a procedure.</Peach>

`POST /organizations/8f2a/archive` is a remote procedure call. It has a target, a
verb and arguments. That is RPC over HTTP, wearing REST's URL conventions.

**This is fine, and you should be able to say why.**

- It is universal. Google's own API design guidance blesses it as "custom
  methods" in AIP-136, using a colon rather than a slash:
  `POST /v1/organizations/8f2a:archive`. The colon is a deliberate signal that
  <Peach>the segment is not part of the resource hierarchy</Peach>, which is tidier than the
  slash and much rarer in the wild.
- The alternative is worse. Modelling every action as a resource gives you
  `POST /archivals {"organizationId": "8f2a"}`, which is more RESTful and reads
  like an accountant designed it. Sometimes that resource is genuinely useful,
  which is <Ref to="#40-designing-a-custom-action">section 40</Ref>'s noun test. Often it is ceremony.
- Nobody is confused. <Mint>A consumer reading `/organizations/8f2a/archive` knows
  exactly what it does</Mint>, which is the actual goal from the first page of this
  book.

So: use custom actions, keep them a small minority of your endpoints, and know
that they are a deviation. <Peach>If more than about a fifth of your endpoints are
verbs, that is a signal your resources are modelled wrongly</Peach>, and the missing
nouns are usually state transitions that deserve to be resources.

<Signal>

"Custom actions are RPC over HTTP. They are pragmatic and near-universal, and
Google's AIP-136 standardises them with a colon separator. I use them where a
state transition has side effects beyond the fields, and I keep an eye on the
ratio, because a lot of verbs usually means a missing noun."

</Signal>

<Trap>

<Rose>"`POST` is open-ended in the spec, so custom actions are RESTful."</Rose>

The premise is right and the conclusion does not follow. <Mint>The specification
permits `POST` to do arbitrary work; the uniform interface constraint still wants
URLs to identify resources.</Mint> The precise position is that custom actions are
permitted by HTTP and are a deviation from REST, and saying so is what separates
someone who read Fielding from someone who read a summary.

</Trap>

<KeyIdea>

<Peach>Custom actions are RPC, they are the right answer anyway, and you should say
both.</Peach> Watch the ratio: many verbs means missing nouns.

</KeyIdea>
