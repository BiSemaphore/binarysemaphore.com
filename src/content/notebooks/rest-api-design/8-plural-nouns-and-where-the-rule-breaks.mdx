The rule, and it is close to universal:

> <Mint>Collections are plural nouns, in lowercase, always, including when you are
> fetching exactly one.</Mint>

```text
   GET  /organizations          list          ✓
   GET  /organizations/8f2a     one of them   ✓
   GET  /organization/8f2a      one of them   ✗   the common mistake
```

The lecture is emphatic about the third line and correct. The reasoning is worth
having, because "because it is the standard" does not survive a follow-up.
<Peach>`/organizations` names the collection, and `8f2a` selects one member of it.</Peach>
You are not naming the organisation, you are naming the set and then indexing
into it. Under that reading the singular form is not a style preference, it is a
category error, and the plural stays plural at every depth.

It also removes a decision. If the rule were "plural for lists, singular for
items", every endpoint would require a judgement, and judgements produce
inconsistency across a team.

**Three places the rule genuinely does not hold**, which the lecture presents as
absolute:

| Shape | Example | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Singletons | `/me`, `/settings`, `/health` | There is exactly one, ever. There is no collection to index into |
| Non-resources | `/search`, `/graphql`, `/metrics` | Not a resource at all. A processing endpoint |
| Uncountables | `/analytics`, `/audit` | The plural is either meaningless or already implied |

`/me` is the important one and appears in almost every real API. It is a
singleton alias for "the resource identified by your credentials". <Peach>The
alternative, making the client fetch its own id and then request
`/users/{that id}`, costs a round trip and leaks nothing useful.</Peach>

<DoThis>

Write the rule down for your team as "plural, except for the three singleton
endpoints we already have, which are `/me`, `/health` and `/settings`". <Mint>An
enumerated exception list is followable; a principle with unstated exceptions
produces one new exception per developer.</Mint>

</DoThis>

<KeyIdea>

<Peach>Plural for anything you can have more than one of, at every level, including
single fetches.</Peach> Singletons and processing endpoints are the exceptions, and
they should be a short closed list rather than a judgement call.

</KeyIdea>
