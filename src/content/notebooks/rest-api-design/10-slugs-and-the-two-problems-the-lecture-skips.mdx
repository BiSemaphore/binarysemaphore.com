A <Pink>slug</Pink> is a human-readable identifier derived from a resource's own content,
used in a URL in place of an opaque id. The transformation the lecture gives is
correct:

```text
   "Harry Potter"  ──▶  lowercase   ──▶  "harry potter"
                   ──▶  spaces to hyphens  ──▶  "harry-potter"

   /books/harry-potter
```

**Lowercase**, because the URL will travel across systems that disagree about
case: some path handling is case-sensitive, some is not, and a mixed-case URL
eventually becomes two URLs for one thing. **Hyphens, not underscores**, partly by
convention and partly because search engines have long treated hyphens as word
separators and underscores as joiners. **No spaces**, which would have to be
percent-encoded as `%20` and become unreadable, which defeats the entire purpose.

That is where the lecture stops, and slugs have two problems that will reach you
within a year.

**Problem one: slugs collide.** Two books called *Foundation*. Two organisations
called *Acme*. Ids are unique by construction; <Peach>slugs are unique only if you
force them to be</Peach>, and forcing them means either a per-scope uniqueness
constraint or an increasing suffix, `acme-2`, which is ugly and leaks how many
collisions you have had.

There is also a collision you will not expect: <Mint>a slug can collide with your own
routes.</Mint> If `/books/new` renders a creation form and a book is titled "New", its
slug is `new` and the two are now the same URL.

**Problem two: slugs change.** The title is edited, so the slug should follow, and
every link anyone saved now points at nothing. Ids never do this. <Peach>A slug is
derived from mutable content, which makes it a mutable identifier, and mutable
identifiers are not identifiers.</Peach>

**Three practical answers**, in the order most teams should consider them:

| Approach | Shape | Trade |
|---|---|---|
| Hybrid | `/books/44-harry-potter` | Route on the id, ignore the slug. Never breaks. Used by Stack Overflow and Medium |
| Immutable slug | Set once at creation, never regenerate | Simple, but the URL drifts from the title |
| Slug plus redirect | Keep old slugs, `301` to the current one | Correct and the most work: you need a history table |

<Mint>The hybrid is the default worth reaching for.</Mint> The id makes the route
unambiguous and permanent, the slug makes it readable and helps search, and
because the server keys on the number, an out-of-date or hand-mangled slug still
resolves.

<DoThis>

If you use bare slugs, add a uniqueness constraint in the database, a reserved
word list covering your own route names, and a decision written down about what
happens when the title changes. <Mint>All three are cheap on day one and expensive
after launch, because by then the answer has to preserve links that exist.</Mint>

</DoThis>

<KeyIdea>

<Peach>A slug is a display convenience, not an identifier.</Peach> Route on something that
cannot change, and let the readable part be decoration that you are free to
correct.

</KeyIdea>
