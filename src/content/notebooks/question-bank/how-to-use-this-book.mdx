This is not a book to read. Every prompt in it already appears inside a notebook, next to the passage that answers it, which is exactly why answering it there proves very little. <Peach>You do not have a knowledge problem. You have a recall-under-pressure problem</Peach>, and the only thing that fixes it is being asked cold.

Each prompt carries the notebook and section it came from, printed small beside its number, so a blank is never a dead end.

Of the 253 prompts, 90 came from an __Interviewer asks__ block and have a written answer in the last part, under the same number. The rest are self-graded: the section named beside the prompt is the answer.

<DoThis>

Work it in three passes

- <Circle>Cold</Circle>. Cover nothing, look nothing up, write what you have. A wrong answer written down is worth more than a right answer read.
- <Circle>Check</Circle>. Compare against the key or the section. Mark each prompt as solid, shaky or blank.
- <Circle>Again, later</Circle>. Come back to the shaky and blank ones only, a few days later. This is the pass that does the work.

</DoThis>

<KeyIdea>

Say the answer out loud before you write it. An interview is spoken, and <Rose>an answer you can write but not say is not an answer you have</Rose>.

</KeyIdea>
