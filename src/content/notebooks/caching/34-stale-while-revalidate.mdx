<Ref to="#17-refresh-ahead-and-stale-while-revalidate">Section 17</Ref>'s pattern, as a header, obeyed by CDNs without you writing anything.

```text
   Cache-Control: max-age=60, stale-while-revalidate=600

   0 to 60s      fresh          serve from cache
   60 to 660s    STALE          serve from cache IMMEDIATELY,
                                 and refresh in the background
   after 660s    expired        block and fetch
```

<Peach>Nobody waits during the revalidation window.</Peach> The user who arrives at second 61
gets an answer that is one second past its freshness date, in the time it takes to
read from the edge, and triggers the refresh that makes the next user's copy
current.

**This is the single most valuable directive for a busy site**, because it turns
the herd from <Ref to="#25-the-thundering-herd">section 25</Ref> into a non-event at the CDN layer: the first request past
expiry starts a background refresh and every other request is served from the
existing copy while it runs.

**Its sibling is `stale-if-error`**, and it is a resilience feature rather than a
performance one:

```text
   Cache-Control: max-age=60, stale-if-error=86400

   your origin returns 500 for two hours
   → the CDN keeps serving the last good copy
   → users see a slightly old site instead of an error page
```

<Mint>For a mostly-read site this converts an origin outage into a stale site</Mint>,
which is a very large improvement for one directive. Both come from RFC 5861.

<DoThis>

Add `stale-while-revalidate` to every cacheable `GET` where a slightly old answer
is acceptable, which is most of them. <Mint>It costs one directive and removes the
worst latency spike your users experience</Mint>, the one paid by whoever happens to
arrive at expiry.

</DoThis>

<KeyIdea>

<Peach>`stale-while-revalidate` means nobody waits for a refresh, and `stale-if-error`
means an origin outage is invisible.</Peach> Two directives, both obeyed by CDNs, both
usually free wins.

</KeyIdea>
