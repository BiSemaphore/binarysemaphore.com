The lecture's account is good. A <Pink>CDN</Pink> keeps copies of your content on servers
placed near users, so most requests never reach you.

```text
   user in São Paulo
        │  DNS resolves to the nearest point of presence
        ▼
   POP (a cluster of edge servers)
        │
        ├── HIT   ── serve from here. your origin never knows       ~10 ms
        │
        └── MISS  ── fetch from origin, store it, then serve       ~150 ms
                     subsequent users in São Paulo now hit
```

<Term name="Point of presence">

A physical location holding a cluster of edge servers. A CDN has tens to hundreds
of them worldwide, and "the edge" means whichever one is nearest a given user.

</Term>

**Three things to add to the lecture's version.**

**The edge cache has a key, and you control it.** By default it is the full URL.
Anything that varies the response and is not in the key means users receive each
other's content, which is <Ref to="#24-cache-key-design-and-the-day-you-serve-the-wrong-user-s-data">section 24</Ref>'s problem at global scale. <Peach>The `Vary`
header is how you add a request header to the edge's cache key</Peach>, and <Ref to="#35-vary-and-the-cache-key-you-did-not-know-you-had">section 35</Ref>
covers it.

**TTL is not your only tool. Purge exists.** Most CDNs let you actively evict a
path or a tag. <Mint>Long TTLs plus purge on change gives you high hit ratios and
fast updates</Mint>, where TTL alone forces you to choose between them.

**Origin shield is worth knowing about.** With a hundred PoPs, a cold object
means a hundred simultaneous misses arriving at your origin. An origin shield is
a designated intermediate cache that all PoPs consult first, <Peach>turning a hundred
origin requests into one.</Peach> That is the thundering herd from <Ref to="#25-the-thundering-herd">section 25</Ref>, solved
at CDN scale.

<Trap>

<Rose>"The CDN routes you to a PoP that has the video quality suited to your
connection speed."</Rose>

The lecture says this and it is not how adaptive streaming works. <Mint>DNS and
anycast routing choose a PoP by network topology, geography and load. They do not
inspect your bandwidth and they do not select renditions.</Mint> Quality selection
happens <Peach>in the player</Peach>: the server publishes a manifest listing every
available rendition, and the client measures its own throughput and requests the
next segment at whichever bitrate it can sustain, switching mid-playback. The
routing layer and the bitrate layer are independent, and conflating them is a
common misunderstanding of how streaming works.

</Trap>

<KeyIdea>

<Peach>A CDN converts a distance problem into a cache problem.</Peach> Control its cache key
deliberately, prefer long TTLs plus purge over short TTLs, and know that an
uncached object means every PoP asks you at once.

</KeyIdea>
