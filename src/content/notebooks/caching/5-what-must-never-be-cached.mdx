A short section, and the one that prevents an incident.

**Anything scoped to one user, in a shared cache, without the user in the key.**
This is the most damaging caching bug there is, and <Ref to="#24-cache-key-design-and-the-day-you-serve-the-wrong-user-s-data">section 24</Ref> covers the
mechanism. The symptom is one customer seeing another customer's data, and it is
a reportable breach rather than a bug.

**Authorisation decisions, for longer than they are true.** Caching "user 44 may
read organisation 8f2a" is reasonable. <Peach>Caching it for an hour means a revoked
user keeps their access for an hour</Peach>, and "we removed them but they could still
see everything until lunchtime" is a sentence you do not want to write in an
incident review. Cache the permission with a short TTL and invalidate on
revocation, or do not cache it.

**Anything you are legally obliged to be able to delete.** If a user exercises a
right to erasure and your caches hold copies with an eight-hour TTL, <Mint>the
deletion is not complete when your database says it is</Mint>. This needs to be an
explicit invalidation path, not a wait.

**Values whose staleness compounds.** A cached account balance used to authorise
a spend is not a display concern. <Peach>Anything read in order to make a decision
about writing needs to be read from the authoritative source</Peach>, or protected by
the concurrency mechanism in <Ref to="/rest-api-design">Notebook 08</Ref> <Ref to="#27-penetration-and-caching-the-miss">section 27</Ref>.

**Rapidly changing values with low read rates.** <Ref to="#1-the-requirement-the-query-that-runs-a-million-times">Section 1</Ref>'s ratio, inverted. All
cost, no benefit.

<DoThis>

Write the staleness window next to every cache in your codebase, as a comment or
a constant with a name. <Mint>`PROFILE_CACHE_TTL_SECONDS = 300` is a documented
decision; `redis.set(key, val, 300)` is a number somebody typed.</Mint>

</DoThis>

<KeyIdea>

<Peach>Never cache identity, permission, or anything you must be able to delete on
demand, beyond a window you can defend out loud.</Peach> The failure mode for these is
not a slow page, it is an incident report.

</KeyIdea>
