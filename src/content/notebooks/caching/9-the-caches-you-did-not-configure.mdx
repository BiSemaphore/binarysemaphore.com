title: "The caches you did not configure"

partTitle: "Where caches live"
---
Before a request reaches your infrastructure it has already passed through
several caches, <Peach>all of which can serve a stale answer and none of which you can
reach into.</Peach>

```text
   the browser's memory cache      this session, very fast
   the browser's disk cache        survives a restart
   the OS DNS cache                name to address
   the browser's own DNS cache     Chrome and Firefox keep their own
   a corporate or ISP proxy        you may not know it exists
```

**The rule that follows is the important one.** <Peach>Once a response has left your
server, you cannot recall it.</Peach> The only control you had was the `Cache-Control`
header you sent with it, and whatever number you put there is now the upper bound
on how long a user may see the old thing. Part VI is entirely about that header,
because it is the only lever you get.

This is why <Mint>static assets are served with long lifetimes and content-hashed
filenames</Mint>: `app.4f2a91.js` can be cached for a year, because a change produces a
different filename rather than a different body at the same URL. The invalidation
problem is sidestepped by never reusing a name, which is the same idea as the
versioned keys in <Ref to="#23-versioned-keys">section 23</Ref>.

<KeyIdea>

<Peach>A response you have sent is out of your control until the lifetime you gave it
expires.</Peach> Hash the filename when you want long caching and instant updates,
because renaming is the only invalidation that reaches a browser.

</KeyIdea>
