Read it in that direction, <Peach>from the painful change backwards to the principle
that would have prevented it.</Peach> Read in the other direction, from principle to
code, it produces speculative abstraction and the pain arrives anyway, wearing a
factory.

Three rules govern the whole book.

- <Peach>A principle is a prediction about change.</Peach> "This will vary, that will not."
  Applying a principle where nothing varies costs you a layer and buys nothing.
- <Peach>Coupling is the disease, and the five letters are treatments.</Peach> If you cannot
  say which coupling a refactor removed, it was not a refactor.
- <Peach>You are allowed to break any of them, once you can say what it will cost.</Peach>
  That sentence is the entire difference between a mid-level and a senior answer.
